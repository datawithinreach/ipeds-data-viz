Five large Boston-area universities admit first-time freshmen with SAT score profiles that look broadly similar at the top — but the bottom of each admitted class is where the schools quietly separate. We pulled the 25th- and 75th-percentile SAT verbal and math scores reported to IPEDS for the 2023–24 admissions cycle. Every score below comes from the same federal reporting form each school files with NCES.

## A familiar ceiling

The 75th-percentile **SAT math** score lands at the top of the 800-point scale at Harvard and MIT and one tick below at the other three schools. Read another way: a quarter of admitted students at each of these schools — even Boston University, the lowest-scoring of the five — has a math score above 780.

The flatness at the top is part of the story: at this tier, math scores compress because so many admitted students score near the test's ceiling. Differences show up further down the distribution.

The wider window is **SAT verbal**. We plot both the 25th and 75th percentiles together so you can see the spread of admitted scores at each school.

Harvard and MIT not only post the highest 75th-percentile score (780) but also the highest 25th-percentile score (740) — meaning even the lower-scoring quarter of their admitted class is at or above the *median* of admitted students at Boston University. The bottom of the admitted distribution, not the top, is where elite admissions distinguish themselves.

## How to read these numbers

These are scores reported by the schools for **admitted students** who submitted SAT scores — not all applicants and not all matriculated students. With test-optional policies still common in 2023–24, a meaningful share of admitted students at each school did not submit SAT scores at all and are not represented in these percentiles. Score percentiles are also a single-test snapshot; institutions weight them differently against GPA, essays, and other factors disclosed in their IPEDS admission considerations.

For a fuller picture, the same IPEDS file also reports applications, admits, and enrolled counts — which we use to compute admit and yield rates elsewhere on the site.
